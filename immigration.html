Immigration

Where the Family Came From and How They Arrived

The Vigliotti, Walsh and Hingston families each crossed an ocean. The records are not all equally firm yet.

Vincenzo Vigliotti was born at Santa Maria a Vico on 4 July 1885. On 1 April 1903 a 17-year-old farm labourer, probably this Vincenzo, arrived at New York on the ship Roma from Naples. The manifest says he paid his own passage, carried $20, and was going to a cousin in Newburgh, New York. The match is probable. Vincenza Pasqualina Guida was born at nearby Arienzo on 21 September 1899, the first of twins.

The 1920 census marks Vincent as having filed first papers only. The 1925 New York State census lists him as a citizen, with a note reading 1923 White Plains, and the 1930 and 1940 censuses list him as naturalized. A Westchester index has a 1916 declaration for Vincenzo Viglietto and a 1922 petition for Vincenzo Vigletti, a probable match. The petition itself has not yet been read. A later 1928 declaration and 1930 petition in the same index belong to a different Vincenzo, born in 1892 at Bonito.

On Barbara's side, Michael Walsh was born at Ardmore, Ireland, in 1837 and settled in Yonkers about 1853. Catherine Jane Cleary was born at Huntingdon, Quebec, in 1845 and married Samuel J. Hingston there in 1872; their daughter Clara was probably born at Port Henry, New York, in 1885.

A related journey is also documented. Pasquale "Pat" Fucale, who married Susan Vigliotti, was born in Cattolica Eraclea, Agrigento, Sicily, on 22 September 1952. At the age of four he and his family immigrated to Montreal, Canada, and about six years later they settled in White Plains, New York.

The Places They Left Behind

Place of Origin, unverified

Santa Maria a Vico, Arienzo and San Felice a Cancello, Caserta

Vincenzo Vigliotti was born at Santa Maria a Vico on 4 July 1885, confirmed by his civil birth act. Vincenza Pasqualina Guida and her twin brother were born at Arienzo on 21 September 1899. Their mother, Antonia Vigliotti, was probably born at San Felice a Cancello about 1866, and died at Arienzo in 1904.

Place of Origin, probable

Ardmore, Ireland

Michael Walsh's 1916 obituary says he was born here in 1837. The county is not named; the map uses the village in County Waterford, the usual place of that name.

Place of Origin, probable

Huntingdon, Quebec

Catherine Jane Cleary was born here in 1845, and she and Samuel J. Hingston were married here in 1872.

Arrival, probable

New York, ship Roma, 1 April 1903

The Roma manifest, sheet 18 line 18, shows a 17-year-old farm labourer, probably Vincenzo Vigliotti, arriving from Naples. He paid his own passage, carried $20, and was going to a cousin in Newburgh, New York.

First home in hand

44 Brambach Avenue, Eastchester

The 1925 New York State census already has the family here, and marks Vincent a citizen with the note 1923 White Plains. The 1930 and 1940 censuses, and 1940s newspaper notices, continue the household at this address.

Related journey

Cattolica Eraclea to Montreal to White Plains

Pat Fucale's obituary describes this Fucale journey. It is on the family map, and is not a substitute for the Vigliotti one.
